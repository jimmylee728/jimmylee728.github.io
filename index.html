Research Program

Much of my work centers on school-based agricultural extension (SBAE)—a movement that transforms rural schools in developing countries into cost-effective hubs for diffusing improved agricultural techniques and raising school attendance, by combining:

  1. inquiry-based science instruction;
  2. extra-curricular clubs;
  3. school farms as demonstration plots; and
  4. student home-farming projects.

Since 2019 I have secured over $4.2 million across 12 grants to pilot and evaluate SBAE in Liberia. Given its demonstrated success, the program is now positioned to scale across rural Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, and Kenya through 2035.

Research Areas

Development economics. I use large-scale randomized evaluations to study synergies between agriculture and rural education, building on the momentum of the rapidly scaling SBAE movement.

Behavioral and labor economics. I develop a framework of endogenous curiosity—people choosing how much model uncertainty to entertain under cognitive limits—with implications for designing interventions that stimulate curiosity about development programs, new agricultural techniques, and educational strategies. It also speaks to the development of human capabilities and to benchmarking them against large language models.

Family and organizational economics. I study intergenerational dynamics in rural household and community decision-making.
